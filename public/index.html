How the readiness check works

Three steps, in your own time

How the readiness check works. You can stop at any point; your answers are saved in this browser, on this device.

  1. Describe how you really work

    Short multiple-choice questions in nine areas, from finance to safeguarding and security. Skip any area that does not apply.

  2. List what you hold

    For each standard document, say whether you have it and when it was last updated. Old documents are flagged, not counted against you.

  3. Take your pack

    Print it or save it as a PDF. Bring it to the assessment, or use it to fill in the donor's form faster.

Privacy

Your answers stay with you

We built Aidfolio so we could never see your answers, even if we wanted to.

  • Nothing is uploaded. The tool runs entirely in your browser. There is no server that receives your answers. If you choose to share anonymous cost percentages, you send them yourself, by email.
  • Saved on this device only. Answers are kept in this browser so you can continue later. On a shared computer, use "Clear my answers" when you finish.
  • You own the file. Save your progress as a file and open it again on any device.
  • Visits are counted, people are not. We count page visits with a cookie-free service. It never sees what you type.

Questions

Common questions

Is this a certification?

No. Aidfolio is a self-assessment. Nobody checks your answers, and the pack says so. Donors and partners will still run their own checks; the pack helps you get ready for them.

Will it write policies for us?

It drafts short statements of what you told it you already do, so you can check, edit and adopt them. It never claims you do something you said you don't. For a full policy, use the draft as a starting point and get advice where you need it.

We have never been assessed. Is this for us?

Yes, especially. Organisations facing their first assessment have the least support and no earlier assessment to reuse. Tick "We have never had an assessment" at the start: your pack then includes an action plan with the dates you choose, so a first funder can see what you already do and what you will put in place.

What is a due-diligence passport?

Some international NGOs now share one assessment of a partner instead of each doing their own, using the open Charter for Change Due Diligence Passporting Tool. The assessing organisation completes and rates it; you review and sign it, and it can then be shared, with your consent, so you are not assessed again. Aidfolio organises your pack by the passport's capacity areas to make that first assessment quicker. Aidfolio is independent and not affiliated with Charter for Change or Humentum.

Who can use it?

Any local or national organisation preparing for a partner assessment: NGOs, community groups, faith-based organisations and associations. It is also useful for an annual check-up.

Which languages are available?

English now. Sinhala, Tamil, French and Arabic are next, translated and tested with local organisations. You can help translate.

How do you decide a document is out of date?

Some documents, like bank statements or board minutes, are only useful if recent. Aidfolio uses simple default periods, shown next to each document, for example 3 months for bank statements. These are our defaults, not an official rule; your donor may use different ones.

Is this legal advice?

No. Aidfolio gives practical, general guidance. Laws differ by country; take advice on legal questions.

Who built it?

Aidfolio is built and run by Richard Prouse in New Zealand as an independent, unpaid humanitarian project. The code is open source, and the questions are free for anyone to reuse. Contact: richard@aidfolio.org.

How is Aidfolio funded?

By small donations, grants, and paid work for international organisations that partner with local ones (see Work with us). Local organisations never pay, and no funder can change what the tools say. Every amount in and out is published in our monthly ledger.
