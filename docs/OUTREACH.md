# Outreach templates

Personal messages beat broadcasts. Adapt each one; never send in bulk.

## 1. To an INGO partnership or localisation lead (email)

Subject: A free tool to help your local partners prepare for due diligence

Dear [name],

I built Aidfolio, a free tool that helps local organisations get ready for partner assessments.
They describe how they work and list the documents they hold. They then get a readiness profile,
prioritised gaps and draft practice statements written only from their own answers.
It runs entirely in the browser: no account, and no data reaches you or us.

It is mapped to the HACT micro-assessment areas and the eight UN PSEA standards, and its pack includes a
summary organised by the capacity areas of the Charter for Change Due Diligence Passporting Tool, plus a
spreadsheet file your assessor can open. It is designed to complement passporting, not replace your process.

Would you be willing to try it with five of your partners and tell me what is missing?
It takes about 30 minutes: https://aidfolio.org

With thanks,
[Your name]

## 3. LinkedIn post (practical value, no hype)

Local organisations receive about 4.3% of international humanitarian funding directly (GHA Report 2026).
One reason is paperwork: proving trustworthiness to each donor, again and again.

I've released Aidfolio, a free, open-source readiness check. It turns how an organisation already works
into a readiness profile, a priority list and draft practice statements, built only from its own answers.
Nothing is uploaded.

If you work with local partners, I would value your critique: https://aidfolio.org

## 4. To the Charter for Change due-diligence passporting group (email)

Send to the passporting contact listed on Humentum's tool page, or to a C4C signatory you know.
Ask to contribute, not to be endorsed.

Subject: Helping partners prepare for their first passportable assessment

Dear [name],

The 2025 overview of the C4C Due Diligence Passporting Initiative, and Christian Aid Ireland's lessons from
Ukraine, both note that the burden falls hardest on smaller local organisations facing their first assessment,
and that partners valued an action plan and a summary of the result.

I have built Aidfolio, a free, open-source tool that helps a local organisation prepare before that first
assessment. It runs in the browser and collects no data. Its pack now includes:

- a summary organised by the eight capacity areas of the C4C Passporting Tool (no self-scoring: ratings stay
  with the passporting organisation);
- a dated action plan the organisation sets itself;
- a spreadsheet export listing every answer and document by C4C capacity area.

My mapping is at capacity-area level, based on the published instructions. I would be grateful for:

1. a check of the mapping against the current version of the tool, so I can map question by question; and
2. one or two partners who are about to have a first assessment and would try it.

Everything I build is released openly (MIT and CC BY 4.0). https://aidfolio.org

With thanks,
[Your name]
